Selection

I used RoCoLe as the base: 1,560 photos of 390 coffee plants (4 photos per plant), with an expert label and the outline of each leaf. A second dataset of arabica leaves (JMuBEN) is a way to test later, after I check its license.

The model does not use the pixels directly. From each photo I take 26 numbers about color, spots and texture. I chose them because rust shows as yellow and orange spots that turn brown, so color and spot numbers describe the disease in a way a simple model can use.

Outliers

I looked for extreme values with the 1.5 × IQR rule.

  • 594 photos (38.1%) have at least one extreme value.
  • Of those, 57.6% are sick leaves, against 49.3% in the whole data. So many “outliers” are real, strong cases of disease (a lot of brown, a very big spot), not mistakes.
  • Decision: I did not remove them. Removing them would delete the sickest leaves. Random Forest does not depend on the size of a value. For Logistic Regression and KNN I standardize the numbers.

Transformation

From each photo I took 26 numbers, only from the leaf pixels.

COLOR

Color numbers

Mean and spread of the red, green and blue channels, and of hue, saturation and value. Share of yellow, orange and brown. The three main colors (K-Means with 3 groups).

SPOTS

Spot numbers

Number of spots, mean and largest spot size, share of the leaf with spots, share of brown, and texture.

SIZE

Leaf size

The share of the photo that the leaf covers.

Other steps

  • Target. The 6 classes became one target: 0 = healthy (791 photos), 1 = sick (769 photos: red spider mite and the four rust levels). This also fixes the imbalance of the 6 classes. Rust levels were kept for the regression and for K-Means checks.
  • Plants. RoCoLe has 4 photos of each plant. I read the plant name from the file name so photos of one plant stay together when I split the data.
  • Scaling. Logistic Regression and KNN use standardized numbers (StandardScaler). Random Forest and Gradient Boosting do not need it.
  • Automatic outline. The app has no expert, so it cuts the leaf by itself with GrabCut. This gave a different result from the expert’s outline, so I trained again with the automatic one (see Phase 4).
  • Same photo, same result. GrabCut has a random part. I fixed its seed, so the same photo always gives the same answer.
  • Speed. GrabCut works on a smaller copy of the photo (longest side 384 pixels) and the browser shrinks the photo before sending it. The analysis went from about 10 seconds to about 2 seconds on my computer, and the accuracy stayed the same.

Training, validation and test

1,248training photos (312 plants)
312test photos (78 plants)
0plants in both groups
  • Split by plant. 80% of the plants for training and 20% for the final test (GroupShuffleSplit, random_state = 42). The 4 photos of a plant always stay together, so the model is tested on plants it has never seen.
  • Validation. Inside the training set I use 5-fold cross-validation, also grouped by plant (GroupKFold). I use it to compare models and settings.
  • No leakage. The scaler is inside a pipeline, so it is fitted again on the training part of each fold. The test set was not used to choose anything. It is used once, at the end (Phase 4).
