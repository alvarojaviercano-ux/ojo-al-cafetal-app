The problem and who it is for

When a coffee plant gets sick, you see it on the leaf first. But we often find the disease too late, and the farmer loses coffee. Many farmers have no expert close by to check every plant.

BENEFICIARIES

Coffee growers

Small and medium farmers who need a quick first check of a leaf with only a phone and the internet.

STAKEHOLDERS

Technicians and advisers

Extension technicians (for example from the Coffee Growers Committee or Cenicafé) who can confirm a result and who define good practice for rust.

STAKEHOLDERS

Course and developer

This is a final project for Machine Learning at UDEC. I built it alone, and the professor evaluates it.

Business goal: help the farmer act on time and lose less coffee, with a web app that checks a leaf from a photo.

From business goals to Machine Learning goals

Business objectiveMachine Learning objectiveLearning task
Find the disease early, from one photoTell healthy from sick leaves, with more than 80% accuracy on plants the model has never seenSupervised, binary classification
Know which disease it isSeparate the 6 classes of the datasetSupervised, multiclass classification
Show where the leaf is damagedGroup the pixels of the leaf by color and mark the damaged groupUnsupervised, clustering
Know how bad it isEstimate the rust level (1 to 4)Supervised, regression
Know what to doLearn which action is best for each rust level and varietyReinforcement learning (in a simulation)

Each question needs a different model from our course.

There are about 26 times more healthy leaves than rust level 4 leaves. A model could learn to always say “healthy”. I fixed this by grouping the classes into healthy and sick for the main model.

Rust level, by how much of the leaf has spots: 1 = 1–5%, 2 = 6–20%, 3 = 21–50%, 4 = more than 50%.

Feasibility: the good and the bad

GOOD
  • An expert checked the labels
  • Photos taken with a phone, just like real use
  • It has rust levels (how bad the rust is)
  • Open license: CC BY 4.0
  • I already use Flask, scikit-learn and Render, so I need no new tools
LIMITS
  • It is robusta coffee, but Colombia grows mostly arabica
  • The photos are not from Colombia
  • The classes are not balanced
  • I only tested it with a few photos from other sources, not a full field study
  • I must give credit to the authors, and say if I change the data
Next step: take my own photos of Colombian arabica leaves (for example Castillo and Caturra) to test the model in real conditions.

License of RoCoLe: CC BY 4.0. I can use and change the data if I give credit to the authors, link to the license, and say what I changed. This does not mean the authors support this project. Dataset: Parraga-Alava et al. (2019), Mendeley Data, doi 10.17632/c5yvn32dzg.2.

Variables, quality and sufficiency

  • Target variable. target: 0 = healthy, 1 = sick (made from the dataset’s binary label). For the other tasks: the 6-class label and the rust level.
  • Variables. 26 numbers made from each photo: color, spots, texture and leaf size (see Phase 2). The photo itself is the raw input.
  • Size. 1,560 photos, 390 plants, 4 photos per plant, 6 classes.
  • Quality. The labels were checked by an expert and no values are missing. The classes are unbalanced (791 healthy and only 30 of rust level 4). The checks are in Phase 2.
  • Relevance. The photos are phone photos of coffee leaves, like the ones a farmer would take. But they are robusta, and Colombia grows mostly arabica.
  • Sufficiency. It is enough for models that use 26 numbers. It is too small for a model that learns from pixels, and it comes from one source, so I cannot say how it works on other cameras without new tests.
